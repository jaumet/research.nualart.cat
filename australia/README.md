# Who Drew These Lines? Australia

*Maps of Australia, from 1788 to today.* A school edition (ages about 10–15), in English, in the style of
[Àfrica esquarterada](../africa_esquarterada/), [Amèrica esquarterada](../america_esquarterada/) and
[Who Drew These Lines? India](../india_esquarterada/).
**First draft written with an AI agent, waiting for review**, above all by Aboriginal and Torres Strait
Islander people.

Open `index.html` in any browser. It works offline and without a server: it loads nothing from other sites.

## The story

One map stays on screen and changes as you read (on phones it sticks to the top). It is told by a
**River Red Gum**, a eucalyptus with a canoe scar on its trunk.

| 1 | Country before the lines | Land with no lines; 16 nations at today's cities | Chips "Whose Country is it?" |
| 2 | A line through the desert (1770–1788) | 1788: New South Wales east of 135° E, the rest not claimed | Share of the continent claimed (50%) |
| 3 | The whole continent claimed (1825–1829) | 1829: 129° E, Van Diemen's Land, Western Australia | |
| 4 | A colony with a promise (1836–1850) | 1850: four colonies | Game "In 1850, which colony?" with 8 places |
| 5 | The frontier wars (1790–1928) | 1850, faded, with 8 places | Chips and dots |
| 6 | The ruler lines (1851–1863) | 1863: six colonies and the Northern Territory of South Australia | The six straight lines; share of straight borders (79%) |
| 7 | Six colonies become one country (1901) | Federation | |
| 8 | A capital and a territory (1911–1938) | 1911: Federal Capital Territory, Northern Territory | |
| 9 | Standing up for Country (1963–1996) | Today, faded, with 6 places | Chips and dots |
| 10 | The map keeps changing | 1788–today, year by year | Play/slider, with the number of colonies, states and territories |
| 11 | Six states and the territories | Today | |

Then: *Your place through time* (32 towns: the traditional owners, then who claimed them on each map),
name cards, questions to talk about, an 8-question quiz, a little dictionary, a section for teachers and
the sources.

## How the maps are made

All maps are cut out of the same land: today's states and territories from Natural Earth. They are cut
along the old lines (129°, 132°, 135° and 141° East; 20° and 26° South; the tip of Cape York) into
17 "pieces" (`build/eras.py`, function `pieces`), and a timeline says which colony, state or territory
each piece belonged to from each year on (`eras.TIMELINE`). Each colony keeps one colour all its life.

- The colonial lines are drawn as they were written in commissions, letters patent and acts. They were
  claims on paper: for decades the colonists controlled only a small part of the land they show.
- **The borders between Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander nations are not drawn**, on purpose (see
  "Open questions"). Chapter 1 shows 16 nations as dots at today's cities.
- The "straight lines" of chapter 6 are the parts of today's inner borders that lie within 0.08° of 129°,
  138° and 141° East, 26° and 29° South, and the Cape Howe line (`geo.RULER`). The percentage is their
  length divided by the length of all inner borders, on this simplified map. The SA–Victoria border is
  really at about 140°58′ East, because of an old survey error.
- Simplified: the Torres Strait Islands become part of Queensland in 1879 (some were annexed in 1872);
  North Australia (1846–1847) is drawn as all the land north of 26° South; Lord Howe, Macquarie and the
  external territories are not drawn.
- Percentages are measured in an equal-area projection (Lambert, centred on 134°E 26°S).

To rebuild the data:

```bash
pip install --user -r build/requirements.txt
python3 build/build.py
```

## Structure

```
index.html        the page (English)
css/style.css     styles (from Who Drew These Lines? India, school edition)
js/content.js     texts of the nations, places, games, cards and quiz
js/app.js         map, chapters, games
data/geo.js       all the maps (generated by build/build.py: do not edit)
build/            geo.py (land, states, projection, straight lines), eras.py (pieces and timeline), build.py,
                  fetch.sh, raw/ (Natural Earth)
tests/            check_static.py, browser.py, run.sh
```

## Tests

```bash
bash tests/run.sh                 # static checks + headless Chrome, at 1280 and 390 px
python3 tests/browser.py google-chrome /tmp/shots   # also saves a picture of the map of each chapter
```

The static checks test the history of the borders (for example: Alice Springs is "not claimed" in 1788, in
New South Wales in 1850, in the Northern Territory of South Australia in 1870 and in Central Australia in
1929; Melbourne is in New South Wales in 1850 and in Victoria in 1851). The browser test checks that every
chapter draws its map, the games, the slider counts (1 colony in 1788, 4 in 1850, 6 in 1863; 6 states and
3 territories today), no horizontal scroll, no console errors and no requests to other servers.

## Open questions

- **Review by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people.** This is the most important one. The names
  of the nations, their spelling, the places chosen, the frontier chapter and the tone of the whole page
  should be checked with the communities and organisations concerned (for example the local Aboriginal
  land councils, AIATSIS, or an Aboriginal education consultative group). Many schools follow protocols
  for this.
- **A map of nations?** The AIATSIS map of Indigenous Australia (D. Horton, 1996) is the best known, but it
  is under copyright and comes with a warning that boundaries are not exact. We chose not to draw one.
  With permission, it could be shown as a separate layer, like the peoples map of *Àfrica esquarterada*.
- **Historical review** of the dates and lines (Geoscience Australia has a page for each border).
- **Translations:** Catalan, like the sister atlases; and Aboriginal languages only if a community wants it.
- **More maps** that could be added: the Torres Strait (1872 and 1879), Papua under Australia (1906–1975),
  the Australian Antarctic Territory.
